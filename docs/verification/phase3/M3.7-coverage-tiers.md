# M3.7 · Coverage tiers and the significance of unpriced items

**Date:** 12 November 2026 · **Owner:** Engineer 1 · **Roadmap:** rev 1.1 M3.7, criterion R1; G3 criterion A4 · **Depends on:** M3.5 (PR #18), M3.6 (PR #19), G3 pack (PR #20)

## What it does

### Coverage tiers (R1)

Every equipment line falls into exactly one tier. The tier is decided from evidence already saved on the line: nothing is recalculated and no AI runs.

| Tier | Rule | Stated confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Source supported | A catalogue correlation used inside its published range, with every sizing input taken or derived from the study's own data | Accuracy of the source correlation and the estimate class |
| User evidence | The user's own price, quote or reviewed comparable | That of the user's evidence |
| Assumption based | Any of: minimum-size floor; extrapolation beyond the range; engineering analogy; a sizing input estimated by AI, assumed, or derived partly from assumptions (including OpenPyTEA coordinates marked `ASSUMED`) | Lower; the assumptions are listed per item |
| Unpriced | No price | None; excluded from every total, not zero |

- **Confidence is stated in words with its basis.** No numeric confidence or probability is given, because none has a calibrated basis (AGENTS.md: no unsupported confidence scores).
- **Source caveats do not change the tier.** These are a correlation not yet checked against its printed source, provisional model scope, and CPI-proxy escalation. They are about the source, not the inputs, and are counted separately.
- **Each tier is reported by item count and by share of priced value.** Value shares are given only when every priced line is in one currency.
- **Assumption-based items are broken down by reason:** floor, extrapolated, analogy, sizing inputs not from the study, and items whose only assumption is a sizing input.
- **The 95% target is checked against source-supported plus user-evidence items:** `meets_coverage_target_without_assumptions`.

### Significance of unpriced items (A4)

For each unpriced item, an **indicative band** is taken from the prices of comparable priced items in the same study:

- **Same classification first.** The band uses priced items of the same classification.
- **Otherwise, all priced items.** The basis is stated per item.

The band is summed and expressed as a share of priced equipment. Under an equipment-factored CAPEX method, fixed capital scales with equipment, so the share also indicates the share of fixed capital.

When an estimate class is available, the upper share is compared with the narrowest typical high range for that class in AACE 18R-97 Table 1 (for example, +20% for Class 4). The result is either `significant` (could exceed it) or `within_class_range`.

The band is **not a price and not a bound, and it is never added to any total.** A test checks that the input lines are unchanged.

| User evidence | 0 | 0% | 0% |
| Assumption based | 26 | 93% | 30% |

Assumption-based items by reason (an item can have several):

| Reason | Items |
|---|---:|
| Minimum-size floor | 14 |
| At least one sizing input not from the study | 25 |
| Sizing inputs not from the study as the only reason | 12 |

Most of these inputs are AI-estimated properties and design parameters: density, efficiency, working fraction, differential pressure, residence time and overall U.

There are no unpriced items, so the significance band is `none_unpriced`.

**This is stricter than the G3 pack's earlier "50% source supported".** That figure counted only whether a correlation was used in range. R1 also asks whether the inputs were assumed. By value, the estimate is mostly source supported, because the largest item (R-101) is in range with study inputs. By count, it is mostly assumption based.

## Founder decision (12 November 2026): strict rule

Question: should AI-estimated **physical properties** (for example a liquid density) count as assumptions, in the same way as AI-estimated **design choices** (for example residence time or overall U)?

**Decision: keep the strict rule.** Any sizing input that is estimated by AI, assumed, or derived partly from assumptions makes the item assumption based, whether it is a physical property or a design choice. An item moves up a tier only when the input comes from the study's data, is supplied by the user, or the item is priced from user evidence.

A future exemption for properties from a cited property method would need that source recorded on the line first. The sizing step does not record it today.
